CS 330: Computational Graphics and Visualization

For my CS 330 project, I created a 3D scene based on a reference image that included a desk lamp, an open book, a mug, and a potted plant. I used basic 3D shapes, transformations, textures, lighting, materials, and camera controls to build the final scene.

How do I approach designing software?

This project helped me improve how I break a larger design into smaller pieces. Instead of trying to create the whole scene at once, I treated each object as its own problem. I built the lamp, book, mug, and plant from simpler shapes and then adjusted their scale, rotation, and position until they worked together.

My design process involved comparing the program to my reference image, identifying what looked wrong, and making smaller changes instead of rewriting everything. For example, I adjusted the mug handle, added a wall behind the objects so the lighting affected the background, and changed the lighting several times until the scene had the warm look I wanted. I can use this same approach in future projects by separating complicated problems into smaller components and improving them through iteration.

How do I approach developing programs?

One of the biggest development strategies I used was iteration. I would build and run the program frequently, look at the result, and make changes based on what I saw. This was especially useful when working with transformations, lighting, textures, and camera movement because small numerical changes could have a large effect on the scene.

Throughout the milestones, my approach became more organized. I reused functions such as SetTransformations(), SetShaderTexture(), SetShaderMaterial(), and the scene setup functions instead of repeating the same code everywhere. I also became more comfortable debugging visual problems by changing one part of the scene at a time. By the end of the project, I had a better understanding of how modular code and repeated testing can make a larger graphics project easier to manage.

How can computer science help me in reaching my goals?

Computational graphics gave me experience with concepts that I had not worked with much before, including 3D coordinates, transformations, camera movement, textures, shaders, and lighting. These skills can help in future computer science courses because they gave me more experience working with larger C++ projects and debugging programs where the output is visual instead of only text.

Professionally, this course also gave me more experience breaking complex systems into smaller pieces and working through problems iteratively. Even if I do not work specifically in computer graphics, those skills apply to areas such as software engineering, AI, simulation, visualization, and other technical development work.
